ThermalPrint draws real barcodes, not pictures of them. Pick the type your scanner, shop, or carrier expects. If you are not sure, Code 128 is the safest general-purpose choice.

Some types check themselves. If you give an EAN-13 or EAN-8 its check digit and it is wrong, ThermalPrint refuses it instead of printing a barcode that would not scan. Give 12 (or 7) digits and the correct check digit is added.

GS1 barcodes

GS1 labels carry elements: an application identifier (AI) in brackets followed by its value. ThermalPrint checks each one before it prints, so a typo is reported instead of becoming a barcode that does not scan.

Other identifiers are refused. In a GS1-128 barcode, a variable-length field that is followed by another one is separated for you with the FNC1 control character. In a GS1 Data Matrix this separator is not available, so put the one variable-length field (batch or serial) last; ThermalPrint tells you if you do not.

The SSCC helper under the barcode settings builds a pallet or parcel number: enter the extension digit (0 to 9), your GS1 company prefix (6 to 12 digits), and a serial reference. It pads the serial with zeros, adds the check digit, and fills the barcode with (00) and the number.

Making barcodes scan

  • Leave quiet space. Keep a blank margin of at least a few millimeters on each side of a barcode. Text or lines touching the bars can stop a scanner reading it.
  • Print large enough. Thin bars are the usual cause of failed scans. Prefer wider objects, and print at your printer's real DPI (203 DPI is common).
  • Keep it black and white. Avoid gray fills behind or inside a barcode.
  • Test a real label. Scan the first label with your phone or a scanner. ThermalPrint does not read a barcode back to test it, and it cannot read barcodes in imported labels.
  • A logo in a QR code needs a bigger code. Keep the logo at or below the limit shown next to the size slider, and make the code at least the width the red note asks for. Test one printed label with your phone first.
  • Data Matrix and QR need to be square-ish. A symbol is drawn as a square in the middle of its box; a very wide box wastes space.

You can use ${date}, ${serial}, and (in a batch) ${Column} placeholders inside any barcode value. See Dynamic text and Batch printing.
