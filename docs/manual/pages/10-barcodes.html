ThermalPrint draws real barcodes, not pictures of them. Pick the type your scanner, shop, or carrier expects. If you are not sure, Code 128 is the safest general-purpose choice.

Choosing a type

TypeAcceptsTypical use
Code 128Letters, digits, and punctuation: any printable ASCII character (space to ~).Product references, SKUs, shipping, inventory. Compact and flexible.
Code 39Capital letters A–Z, digits 0–9, and - . space $ / + %.Industrial and government labels, asset tags. Easy to read but wide.
Code 93The same characters as Code 39.A denser alternative to Code 39.
EAN-1312 digits (the check digit is added for you), or 13 digits with a correct check digit.Retail products outside North America.
EAN-87 digits, or 8 digits with a correct check digit.Retail products too small for EAN-13.
UPC-A11 or 12 digits.Retail products in North America.
ITF (Interleaved 2 of 5)Digits only, in an even number.Shipping cartons and warehouse boxes.
CodabarStarts and ends with A, B, C, or D; digits and - $ : / . + in between.Libraries, blood banks, some parcel systems.
Data MatrixAny text.Very small parts and labels; a square 2D symbol.
QR codeAny text, up to 2,048 characters.Web addresses, contact details, longer data.

Some types check themselves. If you give an EAN-13 or EAN-8 its check digit and it is wrong, ThermalPrint refuses it instead of printing a barcode that would not scan. Give 12 (or 7) digits and the correct check digit is added.

Making barcodes scan

  • Leave quiet space. Keep a blank margin of at least a few millimeters on each side of a barcode. Text or lines touching the bars can stop a scanner reading it.
  • Print large enough. Thin bars are the usual cause of failed scans. Prefer wider objects, and print at your printer's real DPI (203 DPI is common).
  • Keep it black and white. Avoid gray fills behind or inside a barcode.
  • Test a real label. Scan the first label with your phone or a scanner. ThermalPrint cannot test a physical scan or read imported barcodes for you.
  • Data Matrix and QR need to be square-ish. A symbol is drawn as a square in the middle of its box; a very wide box wastes space.

You can use ${date}, ${serial}, and (in a batch) ${Column} placeholders inside any barcode value. See Dynamic text and Batch printing.
