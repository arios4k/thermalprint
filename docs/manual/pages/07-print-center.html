Print Center is where you tell ThermalPrint about your printer. You usually set it up once. The same printer, media, and options are then used by both Import & Convert and the Label Designer.

Print Center has three tabs:

TabWhat it holds
PrinterThe printer setup assistant, the printer and media, output settings, alignment, the label stock preview, and the queue and diagnostics. This is where you start.
Label stockAll your saved stock profiles, for every printer: load, update, or delete one, or save the current setup as a new one. See Stock profiles.
Recent printsYour last 50 prints, to open again or reprint. See Recent prints.

Once you have saved a stock profile, an Active profile menu sits above the tabs, and in the Import & Convert print settings. It always shows which profile is in use, switches to another one in one step, even one for a different printer, and says Changed since it was loaded with an Update profile button when you have adjusted the settings since. See Stock profiles.

Printer and media

ThermalPrint prints through CUPS on Linux and the Windows print spooler on Windows 11. Add the printer in your system's printer settings first. On Windows, configure the label stock in the printer driver so ThermalPrint can list its dimensions. See Windows setup.

SettingWhat it means
Printer queueThe printers your system knows. The line below shows a best-effort hint such as Likely thermal label printer. It is based on the name and media, and is a hint only. A printer that is paused, offline, or not answering shows a note saying so.
Paper or label sizeEvery size the printer supports. A thermal printer lists its label sizes, such as 4 × 6 in; an office laser or inkjet printer lists all its paper, such as Letter, Legal, A4, and photo sizes. Choosing one also sets the output width and height to match. Changing the output size or orientation selects matching media automatically, or custom dimensions when the driver supports them.
Advanced driver resolutionAn optional resolution choice if the printer offers one. Leave it on Driver default unless you have a reason.
Refresh printersLooks for printers again after you add or turn on a printer. This runs in the background, so you can keep working. A printer that does not answer within a few seconds is still listed with a note, so a stuck printer queue never makes the app wait.

Why media matters. The media tells the printer driver how big the label is, so it does not shrink, rotate, or add margins. ThermalPrint keeps the output dimensions and orientation matched to the selected media and sends the page settings explicitly to the Windows driver.

Output settings

SettingWhat it does
CopiesHow many of each label to print (1 to 999).
Rendering DPIThe sharpness the label is drawn at: 203 (typical for 4 × 6 printers), 300, or 600. Use your printer's real resolution. Pages up to US Legal fit at 600 DPI; for larger paper such as A3, choose 300.
Output label sizeThe selected printer's own sizes first, then common label sizes (4 × 6 in, 4 × 4 in, 2 × 1 in, 100 × 150 mm, Letter, Legal, A4), or your own Width and Height in millimeters. Choosing one of the printer's sizes also selects that paper.
Swap portrait / landscapeExchanges width and height.

Label stock and queue

The Label stock card draws your label's shape and size, with DPI, copies, and the printer media. The Queue & diagnostics card shows:

  • A status notice that says what to do next: Add a label, Review the crop, Choose a printer, Choose printer media, Label size differs from printer media, Verify the loaded stock, or Ready to submit.
  • How many imported labels are queued, with Open import queue to jump there.
  • Job status checks the system print queue. When a job is accepted, check the physical output to confirm it printed correctly.

Alignment and printer profiles

Each printer remembers its own settings

If you use more than one printer, such as a 4 × 6 shipping printer and a small price-tag printer, ThermalPrint remembers the media, resolution, DPI, label size, copies, and alignment separately for each. Switch printers in Printer queue and the right settings come back automatically. A printer you have never used starts clean. No "save" button is needed.

Safe margin for a printer that clips its edges

Under Alignment, Safe margin sets how far designs should stay from the label's edge, from 0 to 10 mm. It is remembered for each printer. In the Label Designer, the canvas shades that border and names any object inside it, even when the printer's driver reports no unprintable border. See Safe margin.

Fixing a printer that prints off-center

Some printers feed labels a little to one side, so the content is shifted or the edge is cut off. The alignment offset moves everything by a small amount to correct this.

  1. Print the test label. Press Print alignment test. It prints one ruler label, even if Copies is set higher, with a thin border at least 1 mm from each edge (farther in when the Windows driver reports an unprintable margin), a cross at the center, and tick marks every 5 mm.
  2. Measure. On a correctly aligned label the border is the same distance from each edge. Measure how far it is shifted.
  3. Enter the correction. Set Horizontal and Vertical in millimeters (positive moves content right and down, negative moves it left and up). The range is −10 to +10 mm.
  4. Print the test again and adjust until the border is even.
{{image:alignment-test|The alignment test label: a thin border with tick marks every five millimeters and a cross at the center|The alignment test label, as printed. The border stays inside the printer's printable area.}}

Reset offset sets both values back to zero. The offset applies to printed labels only; exported PDF, PNG, SVG, and ZPL files are never shifted. It also does not apply to raw ZPL printing.

If the whole image is too small or the edges are cut off by the same amount all around, the cause is usually the media size, not alignment. Check that the printer media matches your labels first.

Looking for the label footer? Since version 0.15.0 it is set in the Label Designer and applies only to labels you design there. Imported shipping labels never get a footer. See Label footer.

Recent prints

Batch runs and number ranges are recorded once, with the count in the title, for example Customer label · batch of 50 or Shelf tag · numbers 20; reopening one opens the original design, not a single numbered label.

Each label you print is added to the Recent prints tab of Print Center, with the date and time, printer, and copies. The app keeps up to 50 entries. Search by label name or printer; Clear beside the search field clears the search, while Clear history removes the entries.

{{figure:print-history|Recent prints, with a search field, Open and Reprint buttons, and recorded media, dimensions, DPI and copies|Search recent jobs and choose Open for review or Reprint for a confirmed repeat job.}}
  • Press Open to bring the label back. A design reopens in the Label Designer as an unsaved copy exactly as it was printed; an imported label returns to the queue in Import & Convert.
  • Nothing prints when you press Open. You review the label, then press Print yourself.
  • If the original file has been moved or deleted, you are told to add it again.
  • Clear history removes the list.

A batch print is listed once, as Design name · batch of N. See Folder watching and history for more.

Printer setup assistant

The Printer setup assistant card is at the top of the Printer tab. Choose Set up printer and label stock to start. It has four numbered steps, shown across the top so you always know where you are, and each step has its own controls:

  1. Printer. Choose the printer you will print on. Use Refresh printers if it is missing.
  2. Paper or label size. Choose the size loaded in the printer, from every size it supports: label sizes on a thermal printer, or any paper such as Letter, Legal, or A4 on an office printer. The width and height follow; check the orientation and DPI.
  3. Alignment (optional). Press Print one test label, compare its 1 mm border to the edges, and adjust Horizontal and Vertical. Office printers rarely need this.
  4. Save. Give the setup a name, such as 4 × 6 shipping labels or Letter paper, and choose Save stock profile.

The assistant finishes with a ✔ Stock profile saved page that names the profile and what it holds, and confirms it is now the active profile. Choose Finish to close it, or Set up another to start again for another printer or paper. Back returns to an earlier step, and Close assistant stops at any time without saving; your current settings stay in use.

Stock profiles

A stock profile remembers a printer together with its paper or label size, driver resolution, DPI, copies, offsets, and safe margin. Profiles make it quick to move between printers, or between kinds of paper on the same printer: one printer can have as many profiles as you like, such as 4 × 6 labels and Letter paper on the same office printer.

  • See which one is active. The Active profile menu above the Print Center tabs, and in Import & Convert, names the profile in use. On the Label stock tab, the active profile is marked ● Active.
  • Switch. Pick another profile from the Active profile menu, or choose Load beside it on the Label stock tab. If it belongs to another printer, ThermalPrint switches to that printer too. The label size follows everywhere: an imported label is redrawn at the new size, and the design open in the Label Designer is resized to match (Ctrl+Z in the Designer puts the old size back). Load is unavailable while the profile's printer is not connected.
  • Update. After changing the settings of the active profile, it shows Changed since it was loaded. Choose Update profile to save the changes into it.
  • Save a new one. Under Save the current setup as a new profile, enter a name and choose Save stock profile. The line above shows exactly what will be saved. A green ✔ Saved and active line confirms it. If the printer already has a profile with that name, the button reads Replace stock profile and a warning says so.
  • Delete. Choose Delete beside a profile and confirm.
{{figure:print-center-stock|The Label stock tab with three saved profiles, one marked Active with Update profile and Delete, the others with Load and Delete, and a form to save the current setup as a new profile|The Active profile menu above the tabs, and the Label stock tab. Profiles for every printer are listed; the fictional office printer here is not connected, so its profile cannot be loaded.}}

Up to 50 profiles are kept. If the settings file ever cannot be read, ThermalPrint keeps a copy of it beside the original, named settings.json.unreadable-…, instead of overwriting your profiles.

Reprinting from history

On the Recent prints tab, search by label name or printer. New entries record their crop, page, print settings, and editable design when small enough to retain. Reprint… shows the recorded settings and asks before sending another job. A changed or missing source file requires you to open it for review. Labels moved to Processed keep their history link. Older entries can still be opened for review.

{{figure:history-reprint|Reprint label confirmation showing the recorded printer, copies, dimensions, DPI and media, with Cancel and Reprint actions|Reprint sends a new job only after confirmation. Cancel sends nothing.}}

The recorded printer must still be available. A saved design snapshot can be reprinted even if its original file is gone; imported labels need the unchanged source file. Reprint… is disabled for entries without a complete recipe, including older entries and batch or numbered runs. Use Open and review those jobs instead. Confirmed reprints are added to history. Clearing history does not reset automatic-print duplicate protection.
