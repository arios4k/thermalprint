The Label Designer is for labels you make yourself: shelf tags, price tags, inventory stickers, return address labels, shipping labels, and more. The canvas is the exact size of your label, measured in millimeters, so what you see is what the printer gets.

{{figure:designer-annotated|The Label Designer, with its toolbar, element palette, canvas, properties panel, status bar and sidebar|The current Label Designer, shown with a fictional shipping label.}}

Starting a label

  • Blank: choose File → New label, or Create new label on Home. A blank label is 101.6 × 152.4 mm (4 × 6 inches).
  • From a template: pick one on the Home screen, or open the template menu (the last icon in the element palette). Templates are ordinary labels you can change freely. They open as unsaved, so save them under your own name to keep your edits. Designs you saved with File → Save as template… are listed under Your templates in the same menu (see below).
  • From a saved design: press Open, drop a .thermalprint file on the window, or double-click one in your file manager.

Working on the canvas

  • Select: click an object. Shift+click adds or removes objects from the selection. Click an empty spot to deselect.
  • Move: drag the object.
  • Resize: drag the square handle at the bottom-right corner. It resizes along the object's own direction, even when it is rotated.
  • Rotate: drag the round handle above the object. Hold Shift to snap to 15° steps. For an exact angle, type it into Rotation; fractional degrees such as 23.5° work.
  • Grid and snapping: turn on Show grid to see a guide grid, and Snap to grid to make movement land on it. Label tab → Grid size sets the spacing in millimeters (5 mm to start); snapping moves in steps of a fifth of it.
  • Smart guides: with View → Smart guides on, a dragged object snaps to the edges and centers of the other objects and to the label's center, and magenta lines show what it lined up with.
  • Nudge with the keyboard: the arrow keys move the selection by 0.1 mm, and Shift+arrow by 1 mm.
  • Lock: tick Lock position and size to protect an object. A locked object cannot be dragged, resized, rotated, nudged, or deleted from the canvas until you unlock it.

The line above the canvas reminds you: Drag to move · square to resize · circle to rotate · Shift: 15°.

Notes under the canvas

The bar under the canvas shows the label's name, size, zoom and object count, then the latest status message. Below that, one line shows the most important note about the design, such as an object in the printer's unprintable border, a label size that does not match the printer media, or a footer that was left out. Warnings come first, in amber with a warning sign; information follows in grey with an i. When there are more notes, More notes (N) lists them all, and Hide other notes folds them away again. Buttons that fix a note, such as Match label to printer media, appear right under it.

Saving and the unsaved-changes prompt

Designs are saved as .thermalprint files. They are editable, versioned, and include embedded images, so a design is a single file you can copy or email. The status bar shows Unsaved when there are changes.

Whenever an action would replace your current design (New, Open, choosing a template, a past print, or closing the app), ThermalPrint asks first:

If a save fails or you cancel the file chooser, your design stays open and unchanged.

Your own templates

When you reuse a design, choose File → Save as template…. The design is stored in a templates folder inside ThermalPrint's settings folder, named after the label, and appears under Your templates in the template menu next to the built-in ones. Choosing one opens an unsaved copy, so the template itself never changes. Saving a template under a name you already used replaces it, and the trash button beside a template removes it. You can keep up to 100.

Rotated printing

Labels are usually designed the way they come out of the printer: a 4 × 6 inch label is designed tall, 101.6 mm wide and 152.4 mm long. Some labels are meant to be read sideways, with wide content such as a long address, a big barcode, or a product name across the long side. Rotated printing lets you design such a label on its side, so the text reads straight while you work, and rotates it 90° when it prints so it still fits the label stock the way it is loaded. It is optional and off to start; labels without it print exactly as designed.

  1. Select nothing (click an empty part of the canvas) and open the Label tab of the properties panel.
  2. Tick Rotate 90° when printing. The canvas turns on its side: a 4 × 6 label becomes 152.4 mm wide and 101.6 mm tall. The label stock does not change, and objects keep their positions, so you may want to move them. Ctrl+Z undoes the change.
  3. Design the label. A blue Rotated printing banner across the top of the canvas reminds you that the label is rotated when it prints, and names the size it prints on.
  4. Check the As printed card in the canvas's bottom-right corner. It shows the label the way it comes out of the printer. The label is rotated clockwise to start; if it faces the wrong way for you, tick Rotate the other way.
  5. Print as usual. A note above the print controls says that Print rotates the label 90° to fit.
{{figure:designer-rotated|The Label Designer with rotated printing on: a wide label on the canvas, the Rotated printing banner above it, Rotate 90° when printing ticked on the Label tab, and the As printed card showing the label turned upright|Rotated printing: the label is designed on its side, the banner says it is rotated when it prints, and the As printed card in the corner shows how it comes out of the printer.}}

Rotation applies everywhere the label goes to the printer: Print, numbered runs, spreadsheet batches, Print on a sheet, and ZPL. The size check against the printer media, the printer's unprintable border, and the alignment offset all use the printed label, so the shaded border on the canvas appears on the edges that are actually affected. PDF, PNG, and SVG exports stay the way you designed them. The setting is saved in the .thermalprint file. Auto-length is not available with rotated printing.

If you made a label the size of your stock turned on its side, for example 152.4 × 101.6 mm while the printer has 4 × 6 inch media, the size warning under the canvas offers Use rotated printing. It keeps your design as it is and rotates it when it prints.

Rotated printing is only in the Label Designer. To turn a downloaded PDF or image, use Rotation in Import & Convert.

Labels with no fixed length

On a continuous roll the label should be as long as its content. On the Label tab tick Auto-length (continuous roll): the printed length becomes the lowest edge of any object plus the Bottom margin (2 mm to start), and the status bar and Printed length show the result. Use it with Custom media in Print Center. The canvas keeps the height you set for editing.

Numbered runs and sheets

  • File → Print a number range… prints labels numbered by ${serial}: choose the First number, the Step, and How many labels. Every label is drawn first, so nothing is sent if one has a problem. Use ${serial:4} for zero-padded numbers like 0007, and type a prefix before it, such as INV-${serial:4}.
  • File → Print on a sheet… places copies of the label on an A4 or Letter page for an office printer or a sheet of sticky labels. Choose columns, rows, margin, and gap, or press Fit as many as possible; a small diagram shows the layout. ${serial} counts up from one label to the next. Pages are drawn at the resolution you choose (300 DPI to start; 203, 300, or 600 are offered), and the whole run is drawn before anything is sent. Save as PDF always works (with more than one page, files are numbered -1, -2, and so on); Print sheet needs a printer that lists the paper. More labels than fit make more pages.

If the app closes unexpectedly

While a design has unsaved changes, ThermalPrint keeps a recovery copy a few seconds after each edit. If the app ends unexpectedly, the next start shows Unsaved changes from your last session were found above the toolbar: Restore reopens it as an unsaved copy and Discard deletes it. Saving, or discarding a change on purpose, removes the copy.

The printable area

Most printers cannot print right to the edge of the media. ThermalPrint reads each paper size's imageable area from the printer's driver description (its PPD file) and works out the margin on every side. When the label size matches the media of the selected printer and the driver reports a margin, the canvas shades that border in amber, and the note line under the canvas names any object that reaches into it, because that part may be cut off. Turn the shading off with View → Show printable area.

When nothing is shaded

Many thermal printers tell the driver that the whole label is printable, so there is no border to shade. That is normal, and the app says so: while Show printable area is on and a printer is selected, a short grey line under the canvas gives the reason. Pointing at the Show printable area menu entry shows the same reason. The possible reasons are:

MessageWhat to do
Choose a printer in Print Center …Pick a printer. (This line only appears when a printer is selected, so it is shown in the menu hint.)
Select printer media in Print Center …Choose the paper size your printer has loaded.
Choose media that matches this label's size …The canvas size must match the selected media within half a millimeter. Use Match label to printer media under the canvas, or pick other media.
The printer's driver reports no unprintable border for this media …The driver says everything is printable. If your printer still cuts off the edges, set a safe margin (below).

Safe margin

{{figure:designer-safe-margin|The Label Designer with a two millimeter safe margin shaded around the label and a warning that one object reaches into it|A 2 mm safe margin shaded around the label. The warning names the object that reaches into it.}}

Use View → Safe margin to keep content a set distance from the edge, whatever the driver says. Set it in millimeters, from 0 to 10. The canvas shades that border in amber, and the note line under the canvas names any object that reaches into it. Where the driver also reports a border, each side uses whichever is larger. The safe margin is remembered for each printer, so a small price-tag printer and a 4 × 6 printer can each have their own. You can also set it in Print Center, under Alignment. Leave it at 0 to turn it off. A good starting point for a printer that clips its edges is 2 mm; print a test label and adjust.

Label footer

The optional Label footer adds a small line of text, such as Printed with ThermalPrint or your shop's name, along the bottom of labels you design here when you print them. You can add a small QR code before the message, for example a link to your website. It is off until you turn it on.

Only for labels you design. The footer is added to Label Designer prints, batches, number ranges, printed sheets, and reprints of designs. Imported shipping labels never get a footer, text, or QR code from ThermalPrint, whether you print them directly, reprint them, or print them automatically. The footer setting has no effect on their layout, scale, or output.

{{figure:designer-footer|The Footer tab of the Label Designer properties panel, with the explanation, Print a footer message, the message, Add a QR code before the message, the QR code link, the Left, Center, and Right alignment buttons, and Reset footer|The Label footer controls, with the QR code turned on and aligned right. The canvas shows the footer where it will print.}}
  1. Choose Label → Label footer… in the toolbar, or open the Footer tab of the properties panel.
  2. Turn on Print a footer message and type your Message, up to 80 characters.
  3. To add a QR code, turn on Add a QR code before the message and enter a link or text, up to 200 characters. It starts with a link to this user manual. The QR code prints only with a message.
  4. Under Alignment, choose Left, Center, or Right. The whole footer, QR code included, moves to that part of the bottom edge. Pick a side where your design leaves blank space.
  5. Check the canvas: it shows the footer exactly where it will print. Print one label and scan the QR code with your phone before printing a batch.

Your label never changes size for the footer. The footer goes only into blank space at the bottom of the label, inside the printable area: at least 1.5 mm from the edges, or more when your safe margin or the printer's unprintable border is larger. Turning it on or off leaves the label dimensions and printable area unchanged.

If the footer does not fit, it is left out rather than covering your design, and a note on the Footer tab and under the canvas says what was left out and why:

  • The QR code is left out and the message still prints when there is room for the message but not the QR code, or when the QR code would take more than about an eighth of a short label's height.
  • The whole footer is left out when part of your design is in the space it needs at the chosen alignment. Move objects up, choose another alignment, or turn the footer off.
  • The whole footer is left out when the label is too small for it inside the printable area.

The footer stays small: the message is about 2 mm tall, and long messages are shortened to fit. The QR code uses modules at least 0.25 mm wide and the full white border scanners need. Your footer settings are saved and stay on between sessions. Reset footer turns the footer off and restores the default message, link, and alignment. Exported PDF, PNG, SVG, and ZPL files never include the footer.

Printing from the Designer

Choose your printer and media in Print Center, then press Print in the toolbar. The design is drawn at the printer's resolution (203, 300, or 600 DPI) and sent as a one-page PDF. If a label contains something that cannot be drawn, such as a barcode value that is not valid, printing stops with a message that names the problem. Designs you print are listed under Recent prints in Print Center so you can reopen them later.

For many different labels from one design, use File → Batch print from CSV…. For direct ZPL printing, see Exporting.

When a code overlaps another object

A barcode or QR code needs its bars and the white space around it intact to scan. As soon as a barcode or QR code overlaps another object, the Label Designer warns you, before you press Print:

  • A red Overlap banner above the canvas names the affected codes, and each one is outlined in red on the canvas.
  • Print is turned off while the overlap remains, along with printing a batch, a number range, or a sheet. It turns back on by itself as soon as the codes are apart. You do not need to press Ctrl+Z.
  • The canvas stays visible and editable. Select, move, resize, or reorder the objects as usual until the warning clears.
{{figure:designer-overlap|A rectangle dropped over a barcode in the Label Designer, with the red Overlap banner above the canvas, the barcode outlined in red, and Print turned off|An overlapping code is outlined in red and Print is off until the objects are apart.}}

A code needs its own space even when it is in front, so move or resize the objects until they no longer touch. The Forward and Backward layer buttons keep working while the warning shows, and objects that are not codes can overlap freely, so intentional layering is kept. Every print path checks the design again when it prints, so a damaged code is never sent to the printer.

Checking barcodes and QR codes

Check barcodes checks generated code content, dimensions at the selected DPI, clear space, overlap with other objects, and paper-edge clipping. Invalid or obscured codes also block printing; see When a code overlaps another object. Test the physical print with your scanner to check the printer, stock, and scan conditions.

{{figure:designer-code-check|Small product QR template in the Label Designer with the Check barcodes toolbar action and a successful preflight report below the canvas|Check barcodes reports content, size and clear-space results at the selected DPI. It does not replace testing a physical print.}}
  1. Select the intended printer DPI in Print Center and enter the final barcode or QR data.
  2. Choose Check barcodes in the Designer toolbar.
  3. If a code is invalid or too small, correct its content or enlarge the object. If another object obscures it, move the objects apart and preserve its surrounding clear space.
  4. Keep the complete code inside the label, check again, and scan one physical test print before starting a batch.

The check applies to generated barcode and QR objects. A barcode that is part of an imported photo or PDF is not decoded by this Designer check; inspect its crop and scan a test print separately.
