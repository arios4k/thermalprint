ThermalPrint is a native Linux desktop app for people who print labels on a thermal label printer: shipping labels downloaded from a carrier or marketplace, price tags, shelf labels, inventory stickers, and anything else that fits on a roll. Everything happens on your computer; your files are never uploaded, and you do not need an account with anyone. This manual explains every feature of the app, page by page, with screenshots.

Principles

  • Free and open source. ThermalPrint is released under the MIT license. There is no account, no advertising, and no tracking.
  • Private by design. Labels are processed on your computer. The only things that leave it are the print job you send to your printer and the links you click yourself, such as this manual.
  • Prints through CUPS. If your printer works in other Linux programs, it works here. Nothing is installed or changed on your printer.
  • Careful before it prints. A label that would be cut off is blocked, and a batch is checked row by row before anything is sent.
  • Accessible. Every button has a name for screen readers, and everything works from the keyboard.
  • In your language. The app follows your system language: English, Spanish, German, and French. This manual is in English.

Getting help

  • In the app, press F1 or click the ? button in the title bar to open this manual at the page for the screen you are on.
  • Found a bug or have an idea? Open an issue. Please remove personal addresses and tracking numbers from anything you post.
  • ThermalPrint's author also makes two other free Linux apps with manuals in this same style: Voltkit and Battery Health Center.
