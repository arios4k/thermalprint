Import & Convert turns a downloaded label into something your thermal printer can print at the right size. The label file you open is never changed; ThermalPrint works on a copy in memory.

Where labels come from. Most people import the PDF that a marketplace or carrier gives them after a sale: for example a prepaid label from Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Poshmark, Depop, or Mercari, a label bought from USPS, UPS, FedEx, or DHL, or one from a shipping service such as Pirate Ship, Shopify Shipping, or Amazon. These files are often a full A4 or Letter page with the label in one corner, followed by instructions, a receipt, or a packing slip. ThermalPrint finds the label and crops the rest away. The examples in this manual use these names only to describe where labels come from; ThermalPrint is not connected to, or endorsed by, any of these companies, and it does not buy postage.

Adding labels

You can add labels three ways:

  • Press Add labels… or Ctrl+O and choose one or more files. Alt+← and Alt+→ move between queued labels.
  • Drag files from your file manager onto the window.
  • Set up a watch folder so new downloads appear by themselves. Tick Move printed labels to a Processed folder there to have each printed label leave the queue and move into a Processed subfolder.

Supported files: PDF, PNG, JPEG, TIFF, BMP, and WebP. Each page of a multi-page PDF becomes its own entry in the queue. Files can be up to 256 MB and 45 megapixels. If a PDF is encrypted or damaged, export it again from the place you got it.

The print queue

Everything you add joins the print queue. The queue shows Label 1 of 3 with previous and next buttons, a strip of small thumbnails, and a Queued labels list.

The thumbnail strip appears when there are two or more labels. Click a thumbnail to show that label; the selected one is highlighted and hovering shows its position and file name. A label gets its picture the first time it is shown, so until then its button shows its number. The counters, thumbnails, and the Previous and Next buttons always follow the queue, including after a label is printed and removed.

Each label remembers its own crop, extraction mode, and review state. Recent file names are remembered, so you can reopen files from Home.

Keeping the queue between sessions

The queue is saved as you work. If you quit or the app closes unexpectedly with labels still in the queue, the next start opens Import & Convert with Restore your last queue? at the top of the sidebar. Restore puts the labels back with their crops and shows the first one; Discard forgets them. Restored labels must be reviewed again before you print (unless Skip review for automatic crops is on), and files that have been moved or deleted since are left out. Until you choose, the saved queue is kept and not overwritten.

Use it only for labels whose layout you trust, such as the same marketplace's or carrier's labels every day (for example a stack of Vinted or eBay labels that always look the same). Step 01: Extract

Most carrier PDFs have a label plus instructions, a receipt, or a lot of empty space. Extract chooses which part to print.

Automatic

ThermalPrint looks for printed content and proposes a crop with a small safety border. When one region looks like a label and another looks like an instruction block, it keeps the label and drops the instructions. If several regions look equally likely, it keeps the whole page until you choose. A Queue all N detected regions button turns each region into its own queue entry, useful for a sheet that holds several labels. Automatic is layout analysis, not a carrier-specific recognizer: always review it.

Manual

Switch to Original / crop in the preview. Drag on the page to draw a crop, drag inside it to move it, and drag a corner to resize. The crop is described on the original page, before any rotation. Reset to entire page starts over.

Full page

Prints the whole page. Use it when the file is already a label or when the edges are unclear.

Reviewing the crop

When a crop leaves something out, ThermalPrint asks you to confirm it before you can print. Check the address, tracking number, carrier marks, and the empty margin (the quiet zone) around each barcode, then tick I checked the crop and barcode edges (or Reviewed above the preview). Print all needs every queue entry to be reviewed.

ThermalPrint also warns when the label is shrunk a lot (small text and barcode bars can become unreadable) or when low-resolution pixels are enlarged. Enlarging cannot restore detail that is not in the file.

Step 03: Print and export

  1. Choose the printer. A short hint under it says whether it looks like a label printer. This is a best guess from the name and media, not a guarantee.
  2. Choose the media size the printer advertises, matching the stock you loaded. See Print Center for how media works.
  3. Optionally open Advanced driver resolution to pick a resolution your printer offers.
  4. Set the number of Copies.
  5. Check the line under the readiness message, for example Will print: 1 × 101.6 × 152.4 mm · Printer name · 203 dpi. It shows exactly what will be sent, so a wrong copy count, size, or printer is easy to spot.
  6. Press Print label at the bottom of the sidebar, or the green Print button above the preview, or Ctrl+P. Use Ctrl+Enter to print and move on to the next label in the queue.

Not sure about the paper or alignment? Load plain paper and print the alignment test first; see Alignment and printer profiles. The readiness message above the Print button (Ready to submit, or a warning about the media size) appears before anything is sent.

If the label came from your watch folder and Move printed labels to a Processed folder is ticked, a successful print also moves the file into Processed and shows the next label. Labels you added by hand from other folders stay where they are.

The PDF and PNG buttons save the finished label as a file instead; see Exporting. Job status asks the printer system what is waiting.

The Print button stays disabled until the label has rendered, the crop is reviewed, a printer and matching media are chosen, and nothing is clipped. The status bar and the notice in Print Center say what is still missing.

The preview

The right side shows the label two ways. Use the buttons at the top:

  • Original / crop shows the whole source page and your crop, for drawing and adjusting.
  • Thermal label shows the final result at print resolution, exactly as it will be sent.
  • The zoom buttons and Fit change the preview size.

Under those buttons, a second row keeps the everyday actions next to the label, so you do not have to scroll the sidebar to find them:

The row appears once a label is open. Print all stays in the sidebar, next to the queue, on purpose: printing every label in one click is easy to do by accident, so it is not on the toolbar.

Printing several labels

Add all the files, review each one, then press Print all N labels in order. The labels are submitted one after another in queue order. Unless Keep going if a label fails is ticked, the batch stops at the first problem (a file that can no longer be read, a layout error, or a printer error) and tells you how many were sent. With that option on, only the failed labels are skipped and stay in the queue. Print all uses the printer that is selected when you press it. Paper size, resolution, rotation, margins, scale, copies, printer, and media are shared by the whole batch.

Submission order is not a promise about physical printing. Use Job status and look at the printer to confirm what came out.

If you want to print many different values on one design, such as a list of customers, use Batch printing from CSV instead.

Tips for good barcodes

  • Leave a clear margin (quiet zone) on both sides of every barcode.
  • Keep thresholding off unless you need it.
  • Prefer Fit within paper on a source that is already label-sized.
  • Scan a test label with your phone or scanner before printing a roll. ThermalPrint does not read imported barcodes or measure print quality.
